## Ski poles — need new pair

**Job on Bandet:** thousands of km on flat/medium BC track, deep snow, occasional steep hauls (Lappjord, pulk drag). Poles take **side load** from pulk harness and kick-turns — favour **durable** over ultralight race carbon.

### What to look for

| Feature | Why |
|---------|-----|
| **Large powder basket** (~9–10 cm) | Soft snow, lake ice margins, wind slab — small baskets sink and waste energy |
| **Screw-on baskets** | Carry **2 spares** (same model); easy field swap |
| **7075 aluminium or burly carbon** | Snaps are trip-enders; aluminium bends, carbon can shatter under pulk torque |
| **Length** | BC rule: **~35–40 cm shorter than height**, or armpit-to-ground standing in boots — **not too long** (cold hands). With pulk, many skiers like **145–155 cm** for a 175–185 cm skier — verify on snow |
| **Long EVA grip** | Multiple hand positions (uphill, flats, pulk push) |
| **Carbide tip** | Ice, hard wind crust |
| **Repair** | **Pole repair sleeve** + **Åsnes flex tip** (or spare lower section) |

### Recommended (pick one)

| Pole | Type | Weight (pair) | Best for | Comment |
|------|------|---------------|----------|---------|
| **Åsnes Ingstad Alloy** ★ | Fixed **130–160 cm** | ~450 g @ 145 | **Default with your skis** | Expedition BC pole; **large Hypalon basket** included; 7075 alu; matches Ingstad/Outback system. Buy **spare baskets** + **flex tip** from Åsnes |
| **Start Aluminium** | **Adjustable** (~135–180) | ~550–600 g | Want one pole for flats + steep | Norwegian expedition standard; very tough; large baskets. Good if you are unsure of length |
| **Swix Quantum 4** (or similar touring) | Fixed or adj. | varies | Shop availability | Common in Sweden/Norway; **swap to largest XC powder basket** if stock basket is small |
| **Black Diamond Expedition 3** | 3-piece adj. 85–125 cm | ~520 g | Travel / multi-use | Strong alu + FlickLock; **100 mm baskets** — OK but shorter max length; check it fits your height in ski boots |

**Skip for this trip:** skinny **race** carbon (e.g. Swix Triac), **telescoping trekking poles** without proper ski baskets, poles with **tiny 5 cm baskets**.

### Sizing (Ingstad Alloy — fixed length)

| Your height (boots on snow) | Start with |
|-----------------------------|------------|
| ~165–170 cm | **140–145 cm** |
| ~175–180 cm | **145–150 cm** |
| ~185–190 cm | **150–155 cm** |

Buy from a shop where you can **hold poles in ski boots** on the floor, or order two lengths and return one. On shakedown with **loaded pulk**, if you are constantly reaching or stabbing the basket, adjust **±5 cm** next time.

### Accessories (buy with poles)

| Item | Qty | Notes |
|------|-----|-------|
| Spare **large baskets** | 2 | Same thread as your pole |
| **Åsnes Ski Pole Flex Tip** (or spare tip) | 1 | Field tip replacement |
| **Pole repair sleeve** (~14 mm) | 1 | Splint a bent/broken shaft until exit |

**Verdict:** **Åsnes Ingstad Alloy** in the right fixed length — simplest match for your ski system and 65-day abuse. If you want **adjustability** for mixed terrain, **Start Aluminium** instead.

#### VBL (vapor barrier liner) — discuss

**What it is:** Thin **impermeable** sock or bag (plastic liner, RBH, Baffin liner, DIY bread-bag toes) worn **inside** insulation socks. Sweat stays **inside** the VBL; outer merino and boot lining stay **drier**.

| | **VBL + merino** | **Merino rotation only** (your default?) |
|---|------------------|------------------------------------------|
| **Pros** | Drier boot insulation over **weeks**; warmer in **steady cold** (−15…−30 °C); less ice in boots | Simple; no clammy feel; easier blister management if fit is right |
| **Cons** | **Clammy** when active; **blisters** if wrinkled or too tight; foot hygiene; fiddly in tent | Wet socks freeze stiff; must **dry daily**; 65 days punishes worn merino |
| **Best on Bandet** | Long **no-dry** legs (Padjelanta), **February**, slow pulk days | **March–April**, hut nights with drying, if socks rotated well |

**Recommendation for your trip**

1. **Start without VBL** on shakedowns — **4 new merino ski socks**, rotate, dry in tent.  
2. **Consider VBL** if: feet are **constantly wet**, boots freeze overnight, or you cannot dry socks for **3+ days**.  
3. **If you try VBL:** **one pair** of proper liners (e.g. thin coated VBL socks); wear **thin merino** outside; test **20 km + pulk** before Feb.  
4. **Do not** use VBL as excuse to skip **sock replacement** — dead merino still fails outside the liner.  
5. **Outback** already warm — many pulk skiers use **merino only**; VBL is **optional insurance**, not mandatory.

**Verdict:** **Buy 4–5 new heavy merino ski socks** now. **VBL:** optional — test on a cold weekend; bring **only if** shakedown proves wet-foot problem.
